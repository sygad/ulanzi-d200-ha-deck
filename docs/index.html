Setting up your Ulanzi deck

An Ulanzi D200 is a desk pad with fourteen clear keys over one screen. This turns it into a control deck for Home Assistant: press a key to switch a light, run a scene or change screen, and watch readings from your house on the keys themselves — a temperature graph, a power dial, the time. You lay it out from a panel inside Home Assistant, with no YAML and nothing typed into a terminal.

Six steps, and only one of them happens outside Home Assistant.

You need an Ulanzi D200, a Mac the deck plugs into and leaves plugged in, and Home Assistant 2026.11 or newer. The Mac is not optional: the deck has no network of any kind, so something has to sit on the computer it is cabled to. There is no Windows or Linux version. The deck also has to let a computer in over USB, which mine did with nothing asked for — whether every D200 does, I don't know. Part 1 has the one command that tells you.

1

Download from the Marketplace

In Settings → Marketplace, press the three dots at the top right and choose Custom repositories. Press Add from link at the bottom right, paste https://github.com/sygad/ulanzi-d200-ha-deck and press Add. Nothing else is asked; it works out what it is.

1

Ulanzi Deck is then in the list, as an Integration. Click that row.

1

Its own page opens. Press Install.

2

Restart Home Assistant

Home Assistant raises this itself after any new download. Nothing you have just installed is running until it has restarted once.

If you miss the prompt: Settings → System, the power icon in the top right, then Restart Home Assistant.

3

Add it under Devices & services

Downloading put the files in place; this switches it on. Go to Settings → Devices & services, press Add integration at the bottom right, search for Ulanzi Deck and pick it.

It asks nothing. Press Submit and it is done.

4

Open Ulanzi Deck in the sidebar

A new item appears down the left. Open it and press the one button on the page.

What the connector app is, before you run it

Downloading a program from someone you have never met is a fair thing to be wary of. So here is exactly what it is, and why it has to exist at all.

Your deck has no network. No wifi, no ethernet, no address of its own. Everything it shows arrives down the USB cable. Home Assistant runs somewhere else on your network and cannot see a USB device plugged into a different computer. Something has to sit on the computer the cable goes into and pass messages along. That is the whole job.

What it does

  • Holds the USB connection and passes messages between the deck and your Home Assistant.
  • Tells the deck the time, because the deck has no clock of its own.
  • Copies the deck software across: at the start, and again whenever you update it. Ulanzi's own copy is kept the first time, so you can go back.

What it never does

  • No account, no sign-in, no cloud service. Nothing of yours leaves your network.
  • Talks to one address only: the Home Assistant you pointed it at.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, nothing else running in the background.
  • Never updates itself behind your back. Home Assistant offers a new version; you press the button.

Every line of it is published, the program that runs on the deck included. You can read it, change it, or build the app yourself rather than download it.

5

Open the connector app

Open the zip. Three things are inside: the app, Start here.html — the same four steps as below, with a button that opens System Settings where macOS wants you — and a small pairing file.

5

1. Double-click Ulanzi Deck. Leave ulanzi-deck-pairing.json where it is: that is what tells the app which Home Assistant is yours, so there is nothing to type and nothing to allow.

5

2. macOS will stop you. Press Done — not Move to Bin, however blue that button is. Every Mac shows this for software not sold through Apple's own store. It means nobody has paid Apple to vouch for it, not that anything is wrong with the app.

5

3. Tell your Mac you meant it. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and press Open Anyway beside Ulanzi Deck. It asks once more with Move to Bin in blue: press Open Anyway again, the middle button. That is it, once, forever.

5

4. Press Proceed. The app opens and finds your Home Assistant by itself. Plug the deck in whenever you like — before or after makes no difference. When both lights are green, press Proceed and it sets the deck up in a few seconds.

It also puts itself in your Applications folder on that first run, so it disappears from the download folder — that is meant to happen. From then on it is an app like any other, with its icon in the menu bar beside the clock.

The app stays in the menu bar beside the clock. Closing its window does not stop it; Quit is in that menu, along with the version it is running — listed as Connector app, the same name the panel uses for it.

6

Customise your deck

Back in the same sidebar page. Pick a key, give it something to do, and press Send to device. Preview lets you try a key in the browser before it ever reaches the deck.

7

The screen: when it goes off, and how bright

The gear in the top bar, between the status and Send to device, opens Deck settings. Two things live there, and both are about the deck's screen rather than the keys.

7

Turn the screen off after

A deck showing the same fourteen keys to an empty room is a screen on for no reason, so the deck blanks its own when nothing has happened for a while. 20 minutes to start with, and the dropdown offers 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 45 minutes, then 1 hour, 1 hour 30, 2, 3 and 4 hours — and Never, if you would rather it stayed lit.

Any key brings it back, and that waking press does nothing else: the press that lights the screen never also works a light. Nothing is lost while it is dark — the deck keeps taking updates from Home Assistant, so what you see on the way back is current, not what was there when it went off.

This one is part of the layout, so a change to it reaches the deck when you press Send to device, along with everything else you have altered.

Brightness

The slider runs from about 4% to full. The deck follows your finger as you drag, so set it by eye against the room rather than guessing at a number — the panel is only a few milliseconds down the cable from the Mac.

The lowest setting is dim but never off. Off is what the timeout above is for.

Two things make this one behave differently from everything else in the panel. It does not wait for Send to device: the brightness is already on the deck by the time you let go of the slider, and the deck writes it down itself, so a power cut does not forget it. And Cancel puts the light back — if you drag it somewhere you do not like, Cancel returns the deck to where it was before you opened the dialog.

7

Keeping it up to date

There are three parts — the integration inside Home Assistant, the connector app on your Mac, and the deck software on the deck itself. They are released together and all carry the same number, so any part showing a lower one is simply behind.

A line under the panel always says where all three are. Grey means there is nothing to do; amber means that part is behind, and a bar appears above the line with the one thing to do next — never a list of them, and always in the order they have to happen: the integration first, from the Marketplace, which needs Home Assistant to restart before the new one is the one running; then the connector app, which the integration hands over when you press Update now; then the deck software, which the connector app copies across — so it can only install the version that app is carrying, which is why it comes last.

The app's update is fetched by your Home Assistant and handed to the Mac over the connection it is already on, so macOS does not ask about it again — the warning and the two Open Anyways happen once in the life of the app.
