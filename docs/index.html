Setting up your Ulanzi deck

Five steps, and three of them happen inside Home Assistant. Nothing is typed into a terminal and no files are copied by hand.

What you need, and how it joins up

Home Assistant Runs where it already runs
network
Your Mac The deck plugs into this
USB
Ulanzi D200 No wifi, no network of its own

The deck has no network connection of any kind. Everything it shows arrives down the USB cable, which is why the Mac it is plugged into has a part to play.

1

Download from the Marketplace

In Settings → Marketplace, press the three dots at the top right and choose Custom repositories. Paste https://github.com/sygad/ulanzi-d200-ha-deck, pick Integration, and press Add.

Ulanzi Deck then appears in the list like any other. Open it and press Download.

2

Restart Home Assistant

Home Assistant raises this itself after any new download. Nothing you have just installed is running until it has restarted once.

If you miss the prompt: Settings → System, the power icon in the top right, then Restart Home Assistant.

3

Open Ulanzi Deck in the sidebar

A new item appears down the left. Open it and press the one button on the page.

What the companion app is, before you run it

Downloading a program from someone you have never met is a fair thing to be wary of. So here is exactly what it is, and why it has to exist at all.

Your deck has no network. No wifi, no ethernet, no address of its own. Everything it shows arrives down the USB cable. Home Assistant runs somewhere else on your network and cannot see a USB device plugged into a different computer. Something has to sit on the computer the cable goes into and pass messages along. That is the whole job.

What it does

  • Holds the USB connection and passes messages between the deck and your Home Assistant.
  • Tells the deck the time, because the deck has no clock of its own.
  • Copies the deck software across. Once, at the start, keeping Ulanzi's own copy so you can go back.

What it never does

  • No account, no sign-in, no cloud service. Nothing of yours leaves your network.
  • Talks to one address only: the Home Assistant you pointed it at.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no automatic updates, nothing else running in the background.

Every line of it is published, the program that runs on the deck included. You can read it, change it, or build the app yourself rather than download it.

macOS will stop you the first time. Here is how to get past it.

“Ulanzi Deck” can’t be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software. This software needs to be updated. Contact the developer for more information. Move to BinOK
  1. Press OK. Do not move it to the bin.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Scroll down to Security. There is a line about Ulanzi Deck being blocked.
  4. Press Open Anyway.

That is it, once, forever. Every Mac shows this for software not sold through Apple's own store — it means nobody has paid Apple to vouch for it, not that anything is wrong with it.

4

Open the companion app

One window, and it finds Home Assistant by itself. Plug the deck in whenever you like — before or after opening the app makes no difference. A deck already plugged in is picked up the moment it starts.

If your deck is still running the software it came with, the app says so and offers one button, Set up my deck. Press it once and it takes a few seconds. After that there is never anything to press.

5

Customise your deck

Back in the same sidebar page. Pick a key, give it something to do, and press Send to device. Preview lets you try a key in the browser before it ever reaches the deck.
